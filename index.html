Times Table Strikers

Answer the sum, take the penalty, beat the keeper!

📖 Grown-ups' guide

What it is

Times Table Strikers is a football game that helps your child learn times tables. Every sum answered right is a penalty that goes in. Every sum answered wrong is saved by the keeper.

It follows the National Curriculum for England, from the Year 2 tables up to 12 × 12. It is free, with no adverts, no sign-up and nothing to install.

Getting started

  1. Open andycarter99-byte.github.io/times-table-strikers in the web browser on a computer, tablet or phone.
  2. Type the player's name in the name box and press That's me!
  3. The kit starts as blue-and-white stripes. To change it, for example to sky blue, go to Settings and tap a colour.
  4. Optional: on a tablet or phone, choose Add to Home Screen from the browser's menu. That gives an icon to tap, like an app.

Tap the on-screen number pad, or type the answer on a keyboard and press Enter to shoot.

Spellings

Tap Spellings at the top of the main menu to switch the whole game to spelling, and Times tables to switch back.

  • The words are the official National Curriculum lists for England: the Year 1 and Year 2 common exception words, and the Years 3 and 4 statutory word list.
  • Each word is read out loud, then a sentence with it in, then the word again. The sentence is also shown with the word blanked out. Tap Hear it again to repeat it.
  • Type it and shoot. Right is a goal. If it's wrong, the game shows the correct spelling with the tricky letters in yellow, and the word has to be typed correctly once before moving on.
  • The Season has a match of about 10 words for each part of each list. Every word right wins gold, one wrong wins silver, and two wrong wins bronze.
  • Training Ground can practise a whole year's list or just the tricky words. The Trophy Cabinet shows how many words in each list are known, and which ones are tricky.
  • No sound on this device? In Settings, choose Look, cover, write. Each word is shown for 3 seconds, then hidden while it's typed. The game also does this by itself if the device can't read words out.

How you can help

  • Little and often works best. One match a day, about 5 minutes, does more than a long session once a week.
  • Check the Trophy Cabinet grid now and then. Green means known, yellow means getting there, red means tricky. The game already asks the red ones more often.
  • Start with The Season. Work through the fixtures in order; the next one is marked PLAY NEXT. If the clock is a worry, set it to No clock in Settings.
  • Save the Cup Final for when your child is confident. In June of Year 4, every child takes the Multiplication Tables Check: 25 sums, 6 seconds each. The Cup Final uses the same format, so there are no surprises.

Good to know

  • Progress is saved on each device. Trophies on the tablet won't show on the laptop, so it's simplest to stick to one. Clearing the browser's history or data will wipe them.
  • Updates arrive by themselves. If anything changes, refreshing the page picks it up, and the trophies stay.
  • Sound can be turned on or off with the speaker button at the top. Full screen is next to it. In Settings you can also change the kit and the goalkeeper's shirt colour.
  • Problems or ideas? Let Andy know and the game can be changed.

For grown-ups

This follows the National Curriculum for England: Year 2 learns the 2, 5 and 10 times tables, Year 3 adds 3, 4 and 8, and by the end of Year 4 children should know all tables up to 12 × 12, with the related divide facts. Year 4 pupils sit the statutory Multiplication Tables Check in June: 25 questions, tables 2–12, 6 seconds each. The Big Cup Final copies that format.

The game remembers which sums he gets wrong and asks those more often. The Trophy Cabinet shows a 12 × 12 grid of what he knows. Progress is saved in the web browser on whichever computer or tablet he plays on.
